# Regenerative Civilization: Long-Horizon Game & Platform Spec
**Status:** Draft v0.1 — 2026-11-02
**Related:** [Product Spec](SPEC.md), [Agricultural Observability](AGRICULTURE_OBSERVABILITY.md)
**Scope:** Vision unrestricted by pilot scope. Distinguish proposed from implemented.

## Mission
Build a federated, whimsical, real-world RPG that teaches people to make the world more regenerative, capable, beautiful, resilient and connected. The player levels up by learning and helping; the world levels up through real improvements. The world is explorable, but its change is grounded in physical evidence and community stewardship.

Three linked layers: **physical places and people**, **playable representation**, and **portable commons knowledge**. Each community chooses its rules and privacy. Players can contribute locally or remotely. No particular game engine or stack is presupposed.

## Non-negotiable ethic
- Reality drives scarcity, never fabricated timers or crises. Plant death, seasons, maintenance, crop failures and actual needs matter; do not invent suffering.
- XP is celebratory recognition, not money, labor compensation, governance authority, housing eligibility, food entitlement or proof of competence.
- Consent governs public identity, presence, filming, skill endorsements and federation. Offline/anonymous contribution must remain possible.
- No coercive streaks, induced guilt, fake scarcity, variable-ratio addiction loops, forced networking or leaderboard-based social hierarchy.
- Communities hold local governance, moderation, data portability and exit rights. Accessible, low-bandwidth and non-game UI are first-class.
- Never substitute amateurs or AI for licensed engineering, safety inspection, medical support or public services.
- Respect traditional ecological knowledge, indigenous data sovereignty, sensitive species/site locations, and attribution.

## Domains: an interconnected civilization skill forest
Each includes real objects, measurements, opportunities to learn, and community quests.

1. **Food & seed sovereignty:** gardens, crop rotations, orchards, propagation, native habitat, seed libraries, shared kitchens, nutrition-neutral harvest distribution, postharvest preservation, compost and pest observation.
2. **Soils & biodiversity:** soil health baselines, pH/EC/organic matter, infiltration, earthworms, mycorrhizal research, pollinators, wildlife corridors, habitat quality and restoration actions.
3. **Water & watershed:** rainfall, harvest/catchment, reservoir levels, irrigation, greywater educational resources, leak repair, water testing, drought and flood planning, basin-to-basin collaboration.
4. **Energy & climate:** solar education, consumption, metering, energy efficiency, batteries, microgrid planning, embodied carbon estimates, shade and cooling, weather resilience; professional approval for hazardous implementations.
5. **Fabrication, materials & repair:** tool libraries, maker workshops, electronics salvage, e-waste reuse, spare-part catalogs, repair cafés, durable-design teaching, materials passports and recycling.
6. **Shelter & accessibility:** habitable shared spaces, building upkeep, passive cooling, wheelchair access, safety inspections, maintenance backlog and inviting public commons.
7. **Care & public wellbeing:** community meals, support networks, accessibility, consented skills, inclusion and care tasks; no compulsory disclosure of individual health.
8. **Governance & restorative practice:** transparent proposals, democratic/consent processes chosen locally, community budgeting, conflict facilitation, rotating roles, meeting transcripts only with consent.
9. **Shared provisioning:** ValueFlows-based needs, offers, commitments, surplus exchanges, sharing libraries, expense accountability and equitable access.
10. **Arts & meaning:** performances, music, murals, seasonal festivals, secret gardens, cultural transmission, community histories and playfulness.
11. **Education & research:** mentor networks, courses, workshops, practical evidence, uncertainty, reproducibility, peer review and portable learning records.
12. **Federation & mutual aid:** cross-community parties, guest educators, shared build designs, supply exchanges, reciprocal support, independently governed nodes.
13. **Civic ecology:** publicly useful yet privacy-respecting aggregate statistics, citizen science with methodology, watershed-scale maps, restoration proposals.
14. **Emergency preparedness:** realistic inventory, mutual aid roles, communications drills, backup power, evacuation education and coordination with official guidance; no dramatized disaster gameplay.

## The scale ladder
- **Object:** a seed, probe, tool, compost thermometer; inspect, measure, maintain.
- **Bed/room:** a garden plot, workshop, pantry shelf; understand local flows.
- **Site:** Wild Seeds-like ranch or Castle Quest estate; map presence, events and needs.
- **Community:** diverse people, work parties, governance, projects and celebrations.
- **Federation:** multiple sites with portable skills, shared standards and voluntary collaboration.
- **Bioregion:** watershed and ecology, travel and mutual aid, carefully aggregated data.
- **Knowledge commons:** repeatable methods and empirically tested designs anyone can adapt.
Move between levels without erasing the practical context beneath. At every level, show an inviting 'what can I do?' entry point.

## Whimsy specification
Gardens grow sprite histories, murals commemorate community projects, seasonal birds return, compost sprites become animated by genuine readings, constellations represent past collaborators, and repair stations reveal little stories from salvaged parts. Allow user-made art, mini-games, unusual avatar costumes, and peaceful third spaces. Whimsy is human-created and consented; never falsify recorded ecological state.

## Quest ontology
Quest categories: Care, Observe, Repair, Teach, Learn, Build, Grow, Restore, Prepare, Celebrate, Research, Connect and Govern. Each quest has purpose, sponsor, benefit, location, estimated effort, required capability and safety oversight, accessibility, consent, evidence threshold, verifier and outcomes. Offer participatory roles for beginners, visitors, remote learners, disabled players and nontechnical contributors. Any participant can suggest a quest. Nobody is assigned involuntary obligations.

## Governance of the platform itself
Site delegates and maintainers negotiate shared protocols; federation uses transparent, versioned agreements. Users can export/delete consented personal data (subject to legally necessary records), revoke public presence and challenge misleading endorsements. Governance does not require XP. Avoid making a single founder or platform operator a permanent gatekeeper.

## Failure modes to simulate truthfully, not exploit
Sensor stops reporting -> stale readings and repair quest, never fake values. Harvest fails -> honest loss records and gardening discussion, not user shaming. Crop disease -> local stewardship and qualified advice. Party splits -> respectful cancellation and new commitments. Community withdraws from federation -> retain local operations. A person leaves -> revoke presence, protect their privacy, preserve historical aggregate facts with appropriate consent.

## Open research tracks
Participatory design with growers, Indigenous and locally rooted stewards, accessibility advocates, mutual-aid organizers; agricultural extension guidance; DIY sensor reliability; horticultural climate adaptations; interoperability with existing farmOS/Open Food Network/ValueFlows; verified credentials; offline-first distributed systems; and healthy game engagement. Validate external standards and site policies before implementation.
