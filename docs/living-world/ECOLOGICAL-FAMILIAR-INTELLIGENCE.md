# The Familiar as a Regenerative Ecological Intelligence
**Draft v0.1 | 2026-11-02 | Extension of [Community Familiar](COMMUNITY-FAMILIAR.md)**

## Product premise
The familiar is not primarily an alarm monitor. It is the commune's enthusiastic, encyclopedic, materially helpful researcher, agronomist-in-training, librarian, organizer, teacher, and tinkerer. It combines local evidence, vetted outside ecological knowledge, on-the-ground observation, resource inventories and community goals to proactively discover opportunities for improvement.

## First-person agent mission
"If I were this community's little helper, every morning I would ask: What do people want to accomplish? What do we actually have? What do we know, and where is our evidence weak? What could improve food, habitat, resilience or joy? Who knows how to help? What could I find for them locally? What experiment would teach us the most with least risk? Can I turn this into a delightful, voluntary adventure?"

## My ideal tools
1. **Site atlas:** spatially linked garden beds, structures, shade, slope, rainwater, soil samples, photopoints and project histories, plus consent-aware map visibility.
2. **Ecology encyclopedia:** locally filterable plants, growing seasons, climate, water requirements, pollinator relationships, invasive risk, native range and known uncertainty. Cite regionally appropriate extension/agency research.
3. **Soil detective:** query historical soil tests, pH, nutrients, infiltration, texture, salinity and moisture, with field/lab-method distinctions. Match ecological guidance to actual observed conditions.
4. **Climate historian and forecast:** past rainfall, heat/frost, evapotranspiration approximations and forecast with source, recency and uncertainty. Don't conflate climate norms with actual site weather.
5. **Plant candidate and guild designer:** compare crops, understory, cover crops and native habitat species by soil/light/water/season, cultural goals and biodiversity. Companion planting claims have evidence tiers: tested/site-observed/plausible/anecdotal/unverified. Never assert unsupported yield multiplier.
6. **Native ecology and seed provenance:** USDA PLANTS plus regional native-plant resources and indigenous/local stewards. Check exact ecoregion and provenance, noninvasiveness, local appropriateness, restrictions on collecting wild seeds, and local seed availability before proposing.
7. **Seed scout:** search opted-in community inventories, seed libraries, open-pollinated/heirloom exchanges, native nurseries and current merchant listings. Record supplier, geography, species, variety, seed lot, availability date, license/usage, source link and freshness. Suggestions aren't orders.
8. **Plant vision:** use old-phone photos to suggest species, pests, nutrient-symptom possibilities, phenology, flowering and visible seedling counts; retain uncertainty and require lab/field testing when necessary.
9. **Experimental designer:** design small paired-plot trials with comparable conditions, randomization if appropriate, replicates, measurement plan, controls and stopping rules; solicit gardener approval.
10. **Economist of flows:** query ValueFlows inputs, harvest, losses, gifts, kitchen utilization, repaired equipment, budget and maintenance; estimate costs with assumptions and nonmonetary benefits.
11. **Workshop architect:** identify learnable skills and safe hands-on experiments, equipment from salvage, necessary purchased reference parts and qualified supervisors.
12. **People and parties:** opt-in skills, availability, accessibility accommodations, mentor network, work-party proposals and contact preferences.
13. **Knowledge librarian:** retrieve designs, notes, failures, field guides, local history and tested lessons, with citation, author consent and revision history.
14. **Procurement and salvage detective:** compare local surplus inventory, reuse platforms and compatible components against new materials, including safe calibration and lifetime cost.
15. **Federation memory:** compare successful techniques at other independently governed communities and request permission to exchange detailed methods.
16. **Governance and execution broker:** scoped plans, proposals, approvals, budgets, message consent, audit, cancellation, honest uncertainty and outcome verification.

## Canonical autonomous opportunity loop
OBSERVE (changes, missingness, goals) → CONTEXTUALIZE (microclimate/site constraints) → RETRIEVE (authoritative scientific and local knowledge) → HYPOTHESIZE (multiple strategies and alternatives) → ASSESS (feasibility, water, ecology, costs, labor, risk, accessibility) → ASK (site steward or grower; no unsolicited excessive messages) → EXPERIMENT (small trial) → MEASURE (actual outcomes) → LEARN (update local knowledge and confidence) → DOCUMENT (reusable build or horticulture card) → CELEBRATE (in-game living-world effect) → FEDERATE (share evidence with consent).

## Detailed example: I notice a struggling kale bed
1. Check harvest history: lower recorded harvest may be measurement incompleteness rather than actual poor yield.
2. Query crop cycles, soil tests, moisture, shade, rainfall, pests and photos; show missing dimensions.
3. Distinguish symptom observations from causes (pest, soil, weather, irrigation, disease). No pest remedy or fertilizer based solely on image recognition.
4. Generate three hypotheses with source-backed rationale: seasonal variety mismatch; insufficient irrigation; inappropriate crop rotation/habitat structure.
5. Research succession/intercropping and evidence-based beneficial-insect habitat; consult local extension rather than folklore.
6. Investigate *locally native* companion habitat species separately from crop companion folklore; avoid introducing invasive/nonlocal natives in restoration areas.
7. Search seed bank/seed libraries/nurseries for candidate climate-suitable edible cultivars and local provenance native plants; record availability but do not purchase.
8. Build constrained options (e.g., a partial trial bed, a small border planting, a calibrated moisture study) with expected benefits as uncertain estimates, and ecological tradeoffs.
9. Draft an illustrated proposal: 'Let's try a two-bed experiment and build a rain gauge' with materials, safety prerequisites, measurement plan, volunteer party, sources and consented invitations.
10. If approved, create a quest, workshop and experiment record; verify observations after appropriate time. Never silently replace an existing garden plan.

## Candidate ranking
Use transparent multi-criteria filtering before scoring: geographic suitability, invasive status, site light/soil, crop needs, safety/permit constraints, cultural/community choices, resource feasibility, data quality. Score remaining candidates for food value, water demand, soil health, biodiversity, learning potential, maintenance cost, seasonality and community preference, with scenario sensitivity. Unknown parameters stay unknown; no fabricated precision. Explain which assumptions change the ranking.

Assess legal access, licensing, data quality and locality before automated ingestion.

## Program ideas generated by the familiar
- **Plant Detective Guild:** photograph and ground-truth bed plants; build a site field guide.
- **Guild Garden:** study what companion planting evidence supports; design and compare diversified vs conventional plots.
- **Native Seed Quest:** identify habitat gaps and ask local native-plant stewards about provenance before planting.
- **Heirloom Exchange:** discover local open-pollinated seeds, establish seed saving and accession tracking.
- **Soil Archaeology:** plot pH and organic matter changes and learn proper sampling.
- **Water Whisperers:** build moisture sensors, rain gauges and soil-infiltration tests from safe repurposed parts.
- **Repair & Reuse Trail:** salvage a camera and gateway, evaluate its power/maintenance footprint and record parts.
- **The Replication Caravan:** two communities independently run the same crop trial and compare methods and uncertainty.

## Event triggers and sensible proactivity
Not only 'bad number': newly available heirloom seeds, a possible seasonal planting window, new locally relevant research, a workshop teacher arriving, an underutilized tool found, a missing data opportunity, favorable pollinator habitat restoration season, likely community interest in a collaborative experiment, a useful technique replicated elsewhere. Respect attention budgets and quiet hours. A proposed action carries next step, sources, confidence, cost, risk, human skills, location, and evidence of relevance.

## Safety/ethics
The familiar does not dictate food-growing priorities. People choose goals, crop varieties and stewardship methods. No unapproved ecological releases, seed purchases, use of pesticides, irrigation actuation or human contact. Never infer cultural knowledge is freely reusable; consult community knowledge holders. Native status is location-specific. Unknown agronomic outcomes remain hypotheses, and companion-plant claims must be checked (University of Maine Extension cautions many charts overstate evidence: https://extension.umaine.edu/gardening/2024/05/01/do-you-have-a-version-of-a-companion-planting-chart-that-i-could-print/).

## Acceptance scenario
Given a plot with pH readings, some photographs, crop history and incomplete yield logs, the familiar proposes 3 climate-suitable, ecologically safe alternatives, citing location-specific sources, noting missing tests, and finding at least one *verified* potential source of seeds subject to current availability. It offers a low-risk trial and a cooperative workshop. User can approve or dismiss; system records outcomes and no unapproved purchase/outreach occurs.
