# Mechanics, Motivation and Whimsy: Ethical Octalysis Game Spec
**Status:** Draft v0.1 — 2026-11-02

## Philosophy
Use Yu-kai Chou's Octalysis as a design vocabulary for eight motivations—not as a recipe for extraction. The game asks: what would people *choose* to do if learning, belonging, creativity and visible impact were pleasurable? All dark-drive mechanics must map to honest, material constraints.

## Eight drives and corresponding mechanics
| Drive | Whimsical system | Actual human value | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Epic Meaning | World changes visibly from restoration, communal feasts, water projects and teaching | Purpose and shared stewardship | No guilt about global crises |
| 2 Development | Skill constellations, real project journals and mentor-verified badges | Growing competence and confidence | XP not qualifications |
| 3 Creativity | Build decorations, garden experiments, community murals, new quest lines and instruments | Agency and playful invention | Moderation and ecological safety |
| 4 Ownership | Named stewardship of a garden, adopted repair project, personalized workshop bench | Responsibility and care | No digital enclosure of commons |
| 5 Social Influence | Parties, mentoring, neighborhood welcomes, intercommunity festivals | Relationships, reciprocity | No compulsory exposure or popularity rank |
| 6 Scarcity | Real seasonal seed windows, limited seats and materials | Planning, foresight | Explicit source and no fake FOMO |
| 7 Curiosity | Birds, hidden stories, habitats, rare natural phenomena, community folklore | Discovery and wonder | No manipulative reward schedules |
| 8 Loss/Avoidance | Real spoilage, pests, leaks, deteriorating components | Responsible maintenance | Support recovery, never shame |

## Playable moments
**The Greenhouse Door:** The greenhouse contains tiny animated plants. One looks droopy, corresponding to an observed low moisture reading. On inspection, the visitor sees timestamp and sensor confidence, checks manual ground truth, and joins a real watering shift. Once the intervention is logged, its sprite perks up with a tasteful celebration; the health cannot be asserted based only on watering.

**The Compost Dragon:** A playful heat-loving creature curls on a compost pile, with coloration reflecting measured temperature ranges. It can sleep when data is stale. A beginner tests temperature, logs an observation and learns decomposition fundamentals. No real-world pile is automatically declared safe on the basis of a decorative sprite.

**The Fix-It Familiar:** A salvaged sensor node appears as a curious tiny robot. Its real diagnostics (battery, RSSI, uptime) are inspectable. A workshop party can repair it and publish a reproducible build.

**Season's Ledger:** A communal mural grows motifs for kilograms harvested, food shared, seeds saved, materials repaired, habitat restored and classes taught. Every value links back to anonymized, verifiable aggregate data.

**The Traveling Guild:** Across communities, crews form with consented skill claims. A plumber, artist, teacher, cook and solar mentor together tackle different parts of one shared festival or repair program; quests are structured to value varied labor and accessibility.

**The Observatory:** Wildlife cameras identify candidate bird visits but require human review; a field guide invites observation. Seeing a bird is not evidence it caused damage.

**The Lost Recipe Library:** Recorded techniques, failed attempts, seed experiments and community recipes become explorable library artifacts with creator credit and reuse permissions. Some lore is private or culturally restricted.

## Satisfaction curves
- Immediate: delightful animation; low-friction logging; visible social invitation.
- Weekly: season progress, project completion, learning feedback, recurring care rituals.
- Seasonal: plot comparisons, soil improvements, harvest graphs, community celebrations.
- Long-range: restored habitats, practical expertise, cross-site federation and reproducing successful techniques.
Every curve should offer joyful offramps; no attendance quotas or punishment for absence.

## Social contracts
Discoverable presence is opt-in, expires automatically, and distinguishes 'on site', 'online', and 'open to help'. People can choose 'do not disturb'. Profiles distinguish self-declared, practiced and independently endorsed skills. Parties set boundaries, accessibility needs and decision process; reporting and moderation are available.

## Mechanics prohibited by default
Global leaderboards, attention auctions, coerced quests, exclusive XP-based access to food/housing, artificial resource shortages, misleading timers, real-life emergency spectacle, rewards for photographing unconsenting people, biometric/social scoring, automated camera surveillance of workers, harvest tallies attributed as worker productivity.

## Experiments worth playtesting
- Do players discover a task without a formal training session?
- Do new people find a way to belong within five minutes?
- Can non-gamers reach events and today's needs from one touch?
- Is volunteering celebrated without being judged?
- Do playful representations improve data completeness without pressuring observers?
- Can players distinguish measured data from estimates and fantasy?
- Do players leave sessions with offline actions they actually want to do?
